# Evidence base

The strongest evidence says three things matter most: clear, error-free writing; tailoring to the posting; and signals beyond the CV such as referrals and early applications.

**Grades.** A = peer-reviewed experiment or field study (tagged field or lab; field outranks lab when they disagree). B = large controlled experiment or dataset, not peer-reviewed. C = survey or vendor analysis: direction, not proof. Full citations and links: `rules/sources.yaml` (relative to the skill folder, a sibling of `references/`).

| Finding | Study | Grade | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Better-written CVs get more hires | Wiles, Munyikwa & Horton; ~480k jobseekers; +8% hires | A field | Fix grammar and clarity first |
| Spelling errors hurt as much as missing experience | Lacroux & Martin-Lacroux; 536 recruiters | A lab | Zero typos |
| Plain words read as more intelligent | Oppenheimer; 5 experiments | A lab | Short words, short sentences |
| Referred applicants are more likely to be hired | Burks et al.; 9 large firms | A field | Seek referrals for priority roles |
| Longer unemployment lowers callbacks | Kroft, Lange & Notowidigdo; 12,054 résumés | A field | Apply while employed; keep dates current |
| Local experience raises callbacks for immigrants | Oreopoulos; 12,910 résumés, Toronto; 5.9% to 11% | A field | Show local experience prominently |
| Name and identity cues affect callbacks | Kang et al.; audit study | A field | Inform; disclosure is the candidate's call |
| AI ranking repeats name bias | Wilson & Caliskan; 3M+ comparisons; 85% white-name preference | A lab | Same as above |
| AI screeners favour AI-written CVs | Xu, Li & Jiang; 2,200+ résumés; 68-92% self-preference | A lab | Editor mode by default; cross-model review |
| Tailored cover letters raise callbacks | ResumeGo; 7,287 applications; 10.7 / 12.5 / 16.4% | B | Tailored letter for priority roles |
| Full LinkedIn profile raises callbacks | ResumeGo via Statista; ~24,570 résumés; 13.5% vs 7.9% | B | Link a complete profile |
| Two pages preferred for experienced candidates | ResumeGo; 482 hiring professionals; 2.3 to 1 | B | One page early, two later |
| Most applications arrive early | St. Louis Fed; 56% in first 96 hours | B | Apply within days |
| First scan is short | Ladders eye-tracking; 7.4 s average | C | Design the top third for a fast scan |
| Recruiters search by education, title, certifications | Jobscan survey; 384 recruiters | C | Exact title; credentials in full |
| Heavy AI text is a red flag | Resume Genius 2026; 1,500 managers; 49% | C | Specific, personal detail |
| Action verbs and numbers help | TalentWorks analysis | C | Verb-first, quantify where true |

**Scope.** Most studies are from North America and Europe. The framework applies the same rules everywhere; contributions with evidence from other markets are welcome, tagged by region.
