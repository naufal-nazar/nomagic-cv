# Myths vs evidence

| Myth | What the evidence shows |
|---|---|
| "75% of CVs are rejected by the ATS before a human sees them." | The figure traces to an unsourced 2012 sales pitch (HiringThing). An ATS mostly stores and searches CVs; screening questions do most automatic rejecting (Jobscan). More employers now also rank with AI models, which carry measured biases (Wilson & Caliskan; Xu et al.), so clear, matched content beats tricks. |
| "A CV must fit on one page." | Hiring professionals preferred two pages 2.3 to 1 (ResumeGo). One page only when material is thin. |
| "Aim for a 90% ATS match score." | A match score is a vendor tool's own metric, not a verified employer threshold; none of the sources in `rules/sources.yaml` shows employers filtering on one. What counts is appearing in searches on title, skills and credentials. |
| "A designed CV stands out." | Columns, tables and text boxes parse worst (Jobscan). Recruiters were put off by clutter (Ladders). |
| "Hidden white-text keywords beat the ATS." | Parsed text shows in the recruiter's view; hidden words look like a trick (ATS Verification). |
| "Sophisticated vocabulary impresses." | Needlessly complex words lowered judged intelligence (Oppenheimer). |
| "Cover letters are dead." | Tailored letters raised callbacks about 53% over none; generic letters added little (ResumeGo). |
